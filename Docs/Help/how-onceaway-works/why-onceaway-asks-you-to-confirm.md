# Why does Onceaway ask me to confirm a Pattern?

Because it only sees the shape of the work. You know what the work was.

**Onceaway notices structure; you know what the work actually means.**

## What that means in practice

Onceaway can see that you opened the same two apps in the same order and a PDF
appeared in Downloads, four times this week. It cannot see whether that was
"reconciling supplier invoices" or four unrelated errands that happened to look
alike.

If it guessed, it would be confidently wrong on a regular basis, and you would
stop trusting it. So it asks.

## Your three answers

- **Yes** — these really were the same task. The Pattern is confirmed and can
  be weighed as an Opportunity.
- **No** — they were not. The Pattern is rejected and is never treated as an
  Opportunity.
- **Not sure** — kept, but not treated as confirmed.

All three are useful. "No" is genuinely valuable: it stops Onceaway pushing
something that was never real.

## Does confirming do anything else?

No. Confirming records your answer and nothing more. It starts no action, sends
nothing anywhere, and turns nothing on.
