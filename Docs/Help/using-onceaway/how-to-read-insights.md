# How to read Insights

Insights answers "what has been going on with my repeated work?" rather than
"what did I do at 3pm?". Choose **7 days** or **30 days**, and switch between
**Overview** and **Trends**.

## The four counts

Along the top:

- **Patterns noticed** — repeated activity Onceaway found in the period.
- **Confirmed** — how many you agreed really were the same task.
- **Opportunities** — confirmed Patterns with enough recurrence to be worth a
  look.
- **Want reduced** — how many you said you would like help with.

Read them as a set. Lots noticed and few confirmed usually means Onceaway is
matching structure too loosely for your kind of work — useful to know.

## From noticed to worth reducing

The funnel. Each stage is a smaller group of the one above it, so you can see
where things drop out. A wide top and a narrow bottom is normal and healthy: it
means you are being selective.

## Repeated work by category

Groups what recurred into websites, files, or moving between apps. A quick
answer to "what kind of repetition do I actually have?" If one bar dominates,
that is where any effort is best spent.

## Top repeated activities

Ranked by how often they happened in the period. The obvious place to look for
your first candidate.

## Days with repeated work

How many different repeated activities appeared on each day. Useful for
spotting a rhythm — a Monday spike often means a weekly routine worth naming.

## What's increasing

Repeated work happening more often than in the previous period. This is the one
to check occasionally: things that are growing are usually worth catching early.

## If it looks empty

That is expected at first. See
[Insights looks empty](../troubleshooting/insights-looks-empty.md).

## What you will not find

No score, no rating, no judgement about how your time was spent. Insights
describes; it does not grade.
