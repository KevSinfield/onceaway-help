# How to review and name a Pattern

Reviewing is the one thing Onceaway genuinely needs from you, and it takes a
couple of seconds.

## Reviewing

Open the Pattern from Home or from Patterns. Under **Your review** it asks:
*were these the same activity?*

- **Yes** — confirmed. It can now be weighed as an Opportunity.
- **No** — rejected. It is never treated as an Opportunity again.
- **Not sure** — kept as it is, not treated as confirmed.

Choose, then press **Save**. Your answer is stored locally and survives
quitting. You can change it later with **Edit**, or remove it with **Clear
review**.

## Adding a label

When you answer Yes, Onceaway offers an optional box: *what were you doing?*
Up to 200 characters.

You do not have to fill it in. It is worth doing for anything you might later
want reduction ideas about, because it is the only place your own words enter
the picture.

### How to write a good one

Describe the job in the words you would use to a colleague:

- *Saving images from the browser*
- *Filing supplier invoices*
- *Pulling the weekly numbers together*

Keep it plain. Avoid anything confidential — a client's name, an account
number, anything you would not want in a suggestion request. The label is the
one piece of text that is included when you ask for reduction ideas.

## The name looks wrong

Until you write a label, Onceaway builds a title from what it observed, such as
*TextEdit · TXT created · Finder*. That is a description of the structure, not
a guess at your intent, and it can look odd or truncated.

Write a label and the Pattern reads in your words instead. If the grouping
itself is wrong rather than just the name, answer **No** — see
[When Onceaway gets it wrong](../observation/when-onceaway-gets-it-wrong.md).
