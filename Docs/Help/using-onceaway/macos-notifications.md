# Notifications on your Mac

Onceaway can send you ordinary macOS notifications when something is genuinely
worth your attention. **They are off until you turn them on.**

This is separate from the bell inside the app, which is always there and needs
no permission. See [How to use notifications](using-notifications.md) for that
one.

## What it will notify you about

Three things, each switched on separately in **Settings → General →
Notifications**:

**New opportunities worth reviewing** — a repeat you confirmed has happened
often enough to be worth thinking about reducing, and Onceaway has not yet
asked whether you want help with it.

**Ideas you asked for are ready** — reduction ideas you requested have come
back. This only ever follows something you asked for.

**Assist finished or failed** — an assisted action you approved has completed,
or could not be completed.

## What it will never notify you about

Apps you switched to. Files you created or moved. Websites. Every pattern it is
still thinking about. Observation starting or stopping. And nothing at all
about how you spent your time — there is no score, no streak and no commentary
on your working day, here or anywhere else in Onceaway.

## Turning them on

Switch one on in **Settings → General**. The first time you do, macOS asks
whether Onceaway may send notifications.

- **Allow** — the switch stays on.
- **Don't allow** — the switch goes back off, and Onceaway tells you where to
  change your mind. It will not ask you again.

Onceaway never asks for this permission on its own. Nothing is requested at
first launch, and nothing is requested until you switch something on.

## Changing your mind later

Permission belongs to macOS, not to Onceaway. Change it in **System Settings →
Notifications → Onceaway**. If you switch it off there, Onceaway's own switches
stop working and Settings says so rather than pretending they are on.

Switching all three off inside Onceaway stops everything. It does not touch the
macOS permission, which stays yours.

## What a notification says

A notification can appear on a locked screen, and where and how it appears is
controlled by macOS and your own settings, not by Onceaway. So Onceaway keeps
the text plain:

- the label **you** wrote for a pattern, if you wrote one,
- otherwise nothing specific at all.

It never contains a filename, a folder, a path, a website, an address, page
text, or anything an AI provider wrote. If you have not named a pattern, the
notification simply says Onceaway found a repeated activity worth reviewing.

If a label you wrote is something you would rather not see on a lock screen,
rename it in the app — the notification uses whatever the label currently says.

## Clicking one

Clicking takes you to the relevant place in Onceaway: the pattern for an
opportunity or a suggestion, and the app itself for an Assist result.

Clicking only navigates. It never performs an action, moves a file, or sends
anything to an AI provider.

## When they appear

Only when you are working somewhere else. If Onceaway is the app in front, the
thing the notification is about is already on screen, so it does not interrupt
you with a banner about it.

## Each one, once

An opportunity notifies you the first time it is worth telling you about, and
then not again — including after a restart. Onceaway remembers that it has
told you, and nothing else about it.

## Nothing is counted

Onceaway does not record whether you opened a notification, dismissed one, or
ignored one. There are no analytics anywhere in the app. The only thing kept is
a note that a particular notification has already been sent, so you are not
told the same thing twice.
