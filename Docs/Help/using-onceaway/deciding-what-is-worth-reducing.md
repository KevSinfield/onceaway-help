# How to decide whether something is worth reducing

Once a Pattern is confirmed, Onceaway asks one more question: *would you like
Onceaway to help reduce this activity?*

## The three answers

- **Yes** — you would like help with this one.
- **Maybe** — worth keeping an eye on.
- **No** — leave it alone.

Answering **Yes** records that you care about it. It sets nothing up and runs
nothing. Its only effects are that the Pattern appears under Recommendations
as ready to explore, and that Assist becomes available where it is supported.

## How to decide

Onceaway tells you what recurred and how strongly. It deliberately does not
tell you what it costs you, because it cannot measure that honestly. So the
judgement is yours, and these are the questions worth asking:

- **Is it actually annoying?** Frequency alone is not the point. Something you
  do twenty times a week that takes four seconds may be fine.
- **Is it mechanical?** Repetition that requires no thought is the best
  candidate. Repetition that requires judgement each time usually is not.
- **Would you trust it to something else?** If the answer is no, say no. That
  is a perfectly good answer and nothing pushes back.
- **Is it the same job, or just the same shape?** If confirming already felt
  like a stretch, "No" or "Maybe" is more honest than yes.

## Changing your mind

**Clear preference** or **Edit** at any time. Nothing is locked in, and nothing
was started on the strength of your answer.
